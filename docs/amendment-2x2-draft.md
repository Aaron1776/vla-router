# Draft amendment 76: the 2 x 2 of memory by X-VLA information, pre-registered before Phase 1 labels are opened

Draft by Oscar, 2026-11-26, for Miguel's review. Not approved. To be logged in SPEC §17 **before any Phase 1 label is opened** (labels expected about 2026-11-27 UTC). Items marked **[decide]** need both of us before logging.

**76. §6, §8, §10 (planned analyses; the harness and the four §8 deciders are unchanged).**

(1) **Purpose.** Separate two things the router versus `cascader_nospread` comparison mixes: the value of memory (features built from the robot states both deciders see) and the value of X-VLA's information (features that take X-VLA's commands as an argument). On the Spark's 500 base rollouts, against "the episode fails", both mattered and were about the same size: with the features below, memory added +0.041 [+0.021, +0.060] to the router's step-stratified held-out AUROC, and X-VLA's information added +0.030 [+0.006, +0.054] on top of memory (logistic models, not the shared MLP; `cascader-signal-study.md` and `cascader-intent-screen.md`). The spec's comparison credits the cascader with neither memory nor those X-VLA features. That result is about predicting failure, not about the benefit of a π0.5 call. The analyses below are fixed now, before any Phase 1 label is opened, and are reported whatever their sign.

(2) **Variants.** Two variants are added beside the four §8 deciders, which keep their inputs, profiles and roles (amendment 63):
- `router_hist`: the router's inputs plus the memory features, under the `router` profile (2,075 inputs against the router's 2,062);
- `cascader_hist`: `cascader_nospread`'s inputs plus the memory, X-VLA and plan-phase features, under the `cascader_nospread` profile (2,179 inputs against 2,139).

The `router` and `cascader_nospread` profiles have the same charges in the harness config (nothing at a chunk point, `/features` at a mid point), so their labels, exclusions and remaining time are the same, and `router`, `router_hist`, `cascader_nospread` and `cascader_hist` train on the same rows, labels and weights, with the same model family, hyperparameters, folds, early stopping and λ procedure (§8 as implemented on branch `train-fixes`). They differ in their input columns and in the random seed of their initialisation, which the training code derives from the fold and the decider's name. The equality of the two profiles' labels, exclusions and remaining time is read from the config, not yet run on real labels: it is checked on the records when they are opened, and a difference is reported before anything else is run. Implementation: `src/vlar_train/variants.py` on branch `cascader-2x2` (`0eafe5b`), `python -m vlar_train --variants 2x2`. Every feature is a pure function of the decision-point views so far, at the 10-step resolution of decision points, and is the same offline and live. Checked on the Spark through the real `observe()` on the frozen harness: 4 episodes (3 with scripted escalations), 119 decision points, 17 of them right after a π0.5 segment, 0 mismatches, for the `cascader_nospread` and the router views. Not covered: an abandoned escalation (none occurred); after one, X-VLA drives a fresh chunk and not the proposal the decider saw, `observe()` does not say so, and the features treat that segment as proposed.

(3) **Fixed feature list.** The definitions are the functions in `variants.py` at `0eafe5b`; they do not change after a label is opened. A feature is on the memory side if it reads only the common inputs, and on the X-VLA side if it would change when X-VLA's commands changed while the robot's observed trajectory stayed the same. A segment that X-VLA did not drive as proposed (episode start, a π0.5 segment, a gap in the views) contributes a neutral value: 0 for distances and counts, 1 for ratios and cosines.
- Memory (`router_hist` and `cascader_hist`): `motion` (10 values: net displacement, path length, straightness, rotation change and finger-width change of the end effector, each over the last 30 and the last 60 steps); `fingers` (3: finger width at the previous decision point and its minimum over the last 30 and 60 steps; no threshold).
- X-VLA (`cascader_hist` only): `track_norm` (4: the position and the rotation tracking error of the free signals, each divided by the commanded step of the previous segment, and those two commanded steps); `progress` (4: actual over commanded net displacement over the last 10, 30 and 60 steps, clipped to 0 to 3, and the cosine between the commanded and the actual displacement of the last segment); `stall` (4: the commanded net displacement over the last 30 and 60 steps, and the shortfall of the actual one against it); `vertical` (2: commanded minus actual height at the end of the last segment, and its mean over the last 30 steps); `grip_events` (7: close commands so far, open commands after a close, closes during which the fingers shut, closes that held something and then shut, closes that follow a failed one, whether anything was ever held, and the steps since something was last held).
- Plan phase (`cascader_hist` only): `plan_phase` (6: the number of gripper command changes in the proposal, the step of the first change, the share of its steps commanded closed, that share times the finger width now, the commanded mean step, and the commanded height change).

(4) **How the list was chosen (disclosure).** Everything was chosen on the Spark's own base rollouts (500 episodes, Spark platform, 97 failing) against "the episode fails", held out by task and compared at the same step. No Phase 1 label was read. The choices were made over several passes on that same data and label, so the failure-label figures in (1) are not an independent test of the list:
- `motion`, `track_norm` and the counts in `grip_events` come from the earlier signal study on the same data and label.
- Six further intent features were then screened. Three were kept (`progress`, `stall`, `vertical`) and three dropped for adding nothing (end-of-segment residual, repetition, reversal). The keep rule (adds to the router with memory, interval excluding zero) was stated after the screen's table was seen, not before. The three kept features add nothing measurable on top of the inputs the cascader already has; they are kept as hypotheses about benefit.
- `grip_events` was added to `cascader_hist` after a later pass showed that the commanded-gripper history adds beyond what the finger widths alone show. `fingers` was put on the memory side after the same pass showed that most of the gripper free signals can be read from the finger widths.
- `plan_phase` was included without any keep test, because it is a hypothesis about the benefit of a call that the failure label cannot test. Its contribution on the failure label was seen afterwards (none measurable); it stays in regardless.

(5) **Metric.** The comparison is on the benefit curve, not on failure AUROC. It uses the quantities §8's λ rule already uses (`lam.curve`), computed on each fold's held-out test task from that fold's test predictions:
- **Points.** The test task's labelled points that have labels for branches 1 and 2 and are not excluded under the profile. Every labelled point is one where escalation is offered (amendment 57 (2)).
- **Score and escalation.** A decider escalates a point when p_one_call minus p_continue exceeds a threshold.
- **Gain and rate at a threshold.** Rate: the inclusion-weighted share of the points escalated. Gain: the inclusion-weighted mean over all the points of (b2 minus b1) where the decider escalates and 0 elsewhere (§8's estimated gain per offered point; not the mean over the escalated points only). Weights are w = 1/π (amendment 57 (3)).
- **Curve.** Gain against rate over every threshold that changes a decision, shown up to a rate of 0.5 (the budget's share, the cap the λ rule uses).

The summary numbers, per fold: (a) the gain at fixed escalation rates of 10% and 20%, read from the curve by linear interpolation between the two neighbouring curve points when tied scores make the rate jump over the target (0 gain at rate 0); (b) the gain and the rate at the decider's validation-chosen λ as the training code chooses it, including its rate cap, its episode-support guard and its cross-fitted fallback; a fold whose λ is "never escalate" counts as gain 0 at rate 0, and the realised test rate is reported even where it exceeds 0.5. The thresholds in (a) are quantiles of the test task's own scores: they use test inputs and no test label, and describe ranking quality at equal usage, not a deployable rule. Beside each number: random escalation at the same rate (the rate times the weighted mean of b2 minus b1).

Pooling and intervals: the pooled figure is the unweighted mean of the 10 per-task values, so each task counts once. A task with no usable test point is left out and named. No fold is dropped for having few or no positive points. 95% intervals come from resampling whole episodes within each task (amendments 57 (5), 68 (3)), 1,000 resamples with a recorded seed, the same resampled episodes for all four variants, with each fold's thresholds held at their full-sample values. A task whose points all come from one episode enters every resample unchanged.

Failure prediction is a secondary analysis only: step-stratified AUROC of 1 minus p_continue for "branch 1 succeeded in at most one of its three seeds", on the same points as the benefit numbers (those with labels for branches 1 and 2). Each pair of points is weighted by the product of their two inclusion weights. A point's step is read from its snapshot id (the decision index times 10). The pooled figure is the mean over the tasks where the AUROC is defined; a task with no step holding both outcomes is left out and named.

Implementation: `src/vlar_train/benefit.py` on branch `cascader-2x2` (`2e1083b`), `python -m vlar_train.benefit --out <training output>`. It runs after training and refuses variants that are not on identical test rows, a missing variant, and any labelled point where escalation is not offered. `python -m vlar_train --variants 2x2` first checks that the `router` and `cascader_nospread` profiles give identical labels, exclusions and remaining time at every point, and stops if they do not.

(6) **Contrasts**, each paired on the same test points and resampled episodes, all reported for each summary number in (5):
- memory: `router_hist` minus `router`;
- X-VLA information given memory: `cascader_hist` minus `router_hist` (both sides have memory, so the difference is the X-VLA and plan-phase features and the spec's cascader inputs);
- X-VLA information without memory: `cascader_nospread` minus `router`;
- interaction: the first X-VLA contrast minus the second.

**Primary contrast:** X-VLA information given memory (`cascader_hist` minus `router_hist`), gain at the 10% escalation rate, pooled as in (5). It is named before any label is opened and answers the study's question (the value of seeing X-VLA's commands) with memory held equal. Every other contrast and summary number is secondary; no correction for multiple comparisons is applied to them, every interval is reported, and the report says how many there are.

(7) **What this does not change.** The headline comparison stays `cascader_nospread` versus `router` (amendment 63), and the harness stays frozen. **[decide]** Whether the router may keep history in the headline (open; the 2 x 2 reports both ways), and which variants are evaluated live and drive the DAgger round (§9): the two §8 headline deciders only, or also `router_hist` and `cascader_hist`.

(8) **After labels are opened.** No feature is added, removed or redefined, and the metric, contrasts and pooling in (5) and (6) stay as written. A bug fix that changes any reported number is logged in §17 with the numbers before and after. Any analysis not listed here (other rates, other models, single-feature ablations, per-task breakdowns) is reported as exploratory and labelled as such.
